Every claim names its source. Page numbers are the printed ones of UG576 v1.7.1 (18 Aug 2021, local copy). **UNCONFIRMED** marks anything not read in a document.

## 1. XAPP1276 (FRACXO): can loop timing be done, and does it need buffer bypass or a TXUSRCLK from the recovered clock?

**Finding that changes 96F's V1.** XAPP1276 does **not** drive `TXUSRCLK` from the recovered clock. The single clock is the **transmit** one, taken from the QPLL: the fabric clock is `TXOUTCLK` through one `BUFG_GT` (Figures 2, 12, 13, 15; the text says, in my translation, that the FRACXO clock is derived from `TXOUTCLK` and shared with `TXUSERCLK`). A digital PLL in the fabric (the "FRACXO DPLL") compares that clock with the **recovered clock** and writes the QPLL's SDM word, so the QPLL (the transmit line rate) is pulled to the recovered rate. Recovered clock goes only to the DPLL's phase detector (`REF_CLK_I`: "any clock: local, BUFG, pulse"). The note is built around a **loop-timed 10.3125 Gb/s link on a GTY (VCU108)** and says the reference design is "the Dynamic Frac-N example recommended in UG578" ready to use; it covers "Virtex UltraScale GTY, Kintex/Virtex UltraScale+ and Zynq UltraScale+ GTH, GTM and GTY" (abstract; Table 1 notes `SDM_TOGGLE_O` is for the UltraScale+ GTH, GTM and GTY only; Table 5 gives utilisation for one GTH on a Zynq UltraScale+).

**XAPP1276 never mentions buffer bypass.** (Searched the full text: no "bypass", no `TXBUF_EN`, no `RXBUF_EN`.) Its example runs the transceiver as the Wizard's default and measures PRBS error-free loop timing and jitter. The latency claim for bypass plus FRACXO is made by AMD's blog (below), not by XAPP1276.

**AMD's tested low-latency recipe (blog, GTY on Alveo U200, 10.3125 Gb/s).** "RX and TX buffers bypassed", "make TXOUTCLK master of TXUSRCLKs/RXUSRCLKs", set `RXSYNC_SKIP_DA = 1'b1` "to operate RX as a slave and skip delay alignment", FRACXO `REF_CLK_I` driven by a `BUFG_GT` fed from the **receiver's `RXOUTCLK`**. In other words: one clock net (TX-derived) for TX and RX user clocks; the recovered clock feeds only the phase detector. This is the same arrangement UG576 documents for multi-lane GTH ("TXUSRCLK and RXUSRCLK Sharing Using Both TX and RX Buffer Bypass", p.151): all lanes share the buffer-bypass master's `TXOUTCLK`, "all slave lanes must skip delay alignment", "the RX master channel should also set `RXSYNC_SKIP_DA = 1'b1`" (`RXSYNC_SKIP_DA` attribute, Table 4-30, p.259: "only valid on RX buffer bypass master lane in auto mode"). The blog reports 274 UI (26.57 ns calculated, 27.83 ns measured round trip RX to TX) with a 32-bit path, 182 UI (17.65 / 18.43 ns) at 20 bits and 152 UI (14.74 / 14.83 ns) at 16 bits.
Why AMD needs it: "XAPP1276, or FRACXO, is required to lock the receive clock to the transmit clock reducing the clock domain crossing latency to single fabric clock period."

**Answer to the brief's question.** V1 as 96F described it (TXUSRCLK2 and RXUSRCLK2 both the recovered clock) is **not what AMD does and is not documented**. The documented and tested form of "one clock for the whole fabric" is: **TXOUTCLK (QPLL) is the one clock; the DPLL pulls the QPLL onto the recovered rate; RXUSRCLK = TXUSRCLK; RX bypass with `RXSYNC_SKIP_DA = 1`.** Call that V1'. It is supported by hardware evidence on a GTY and documented for GTH only as the multi-lane sharing rule; **the single-lane GTH with `RXSYNC_SKIP_DA` is UNCONFIRMED** (the blog says the latency "will be identical on any UltraScale Plus transceiver": AMD's claim about latency, not a statement about the GTH sequence).

What it requires (all from XAPP1276 unless noted):
- QPLL0 or QPLL1 clocks the channel; `TXPLLCLKSEL` = 10 or 11 (p.18); SDM function and ports enabled when the wrapper and example design are generated; DRC (`opt_design`) warns when not; **no DRC checks the reference frequency** (p.19), so the nominal rate must be chosen by hand.
- Nominal rate not an integer multiple of the reference, so that the fractional range can go both ways (p.3). Their UltraScale+ answer is the signed `Volt` added to `CENTRE_F` (Figure 13), limited by `SDM_MIN`/`SDM_MAX`; the blog centres the fractional QPLL at N + 0.5 with a programmable Si570 (155.075188 MHz, 10.3125 G / 66.5).
- After a QPLL reset the dynamic SDM excursion must stay within **+-200 ppm** of the nominal (UltraScale and UltraScale+; p.3, p.8). UltraScale only: the dynamic range is 1/64 of N to N+1 with the lower 18 bits adjusted (not UltraScale+: "no sub-tuning band").
- Fabric: `TXOUTCLK_I` and `REF_CLK_I` need period constraints; the transceiver gets a LOC constraint (p.18). The DPLL runs on `TXOUTCLK` and a slower `CE_DSP` enable; reset the DPLL 8 `TXOUTCLK` cycles, **after** the QPLL and TX PMA reset sequence has finished (p.14). **Simulation of the example is "not supported"** (p.19).
- Loop (p.3 to 5, 10 to 13): 2nd-order digital PI loop. Oversampling PFD (low noise, wide range) whose error is accumulated over a `CE_DSP` window; `G1` (linear, range -8 to 20) and `G2` (integrator, 0 to 20, always larger than `G1` for stability), `RANGE` 0 to 7 (UltraScale+), `R` and `V` integer dividers so both sides compare at the same frequency, typically "2 MHz or less", `CE_DSP_RATE` default 0x0007FF. Recommended bandwidth **below 1 kHz** (jitter and cleaning 0.1 Hz to 1 kHz); a higher bandwidth is possible at the cost of jitter. **"Lock with a high bandwidth, clean with a low one"**: `G1`/`G2` can be changed without losing lock, watching the DPLL's error output. `HOLD` freezes the filter output (use when the reference vanishes); `OFFSET_PPM`/`OFFSET_EN` override the loop output (UltraScale GTY only); `DISABLE` forces `CENTRE_F` (UltraScale+); `DON_I` dithers the PFD to linearise it. A spreadsheet in the design package predicts the transfer function (not retrieved).
- Cost: the stand-alone DPLL is about 763 LUT and 919 registers (Table 6, as AMD measured it with the tools of the time); the full example with one GTH is 3,087 LUT and 4,514 registers (Table 5).

**Implications for the 96F model.** Compatible: a PI loop, an update far above the loop bandwidth, a sub-clock phase detector (AMD's PFD is oversampled; our "one-clock detector holds only +-3 ns" finding says the same), a high-bandwidth lock followed by a narrow band (our FLL then PI), holdover by freezing the output, the 200 ppm rule. Different: AMD's recommended loop bandwidth is **below 1 kHz** against our 30 to 100 Hz (inside it); its update rate is the TX parallel-clock rate divided by `CE_DSP_RATE`, not 10 kHz; AMD pulls the QPLL by **adding a signed correction to a centre** and has no one-sided window. XAPP1276 has no answer for our fixed 156.25 MHz reference (below).

## 2. The Transceiver Wizard example design: how the bypass controllers are driven (PG182; generated from Vivado 2026.1)

Method: `results/b96f2/probe5.tcl` builds the b96 core (the `rm_gp_bypass` configuration, in-core QPLL and `LOCATE_COMMON = EXAMPLE_DESIGN`), calls `open_example_project` and stops. Result: example projects for both variants (11 and 13 generated source files); the example top, wrapper, init, reset-synchroniser and constraint files were read. No synthesis, no implementation, no board. The generated files carry AMD's copyright header and are not committed.

**TX controller.** `gtwiz_buffbypass_tx_reset_in` = a 5-flop synchroniser (asynchronous assert, synchronous release, `ASYNC_REG`) clocked by `TXUSRCLK2`, from `~gtwiz_userclk_tx_active_out`: "held in reset until the TX user clocking network helper block which drives it is active" (PG182 helper-block port page: "pulse … immediately after TXUSRCLK2 stabilizes"). `start_user_in` is tied **0** (the controller restarts itself on the rising edge of the TX reset-done input and on `start_user_in`).
**RX controller.** `reset_in` = the same synchroniser on `RXUSRCLK2`, from `~gtwiz_userclk_rx_active_out || ~gtwiz_buffbypass_tx_done_out`; AMD's comment says the second term is "for this loopback configuration" (RX waits for TX). `start_user_in` tied 0.
**Done and error.** Neither is acted on by the design file as a gate. `done` is ANDed with the reset helper's done into the example's `tx_init_done` and `rx_init_done`; the init state machine (`example_init`) times them: **30 ms for TX init, 130 ms for RX init** (`P_TX_TIMER_DURATION_US` 30,000, `P_RX_TIMER_DURATION_US` 130,000; default parameters) and, if TX is not done, asserts `reset_all` and retries (retry counter saturating at 15); if RX is not done or the data check fails, it pulses a receiver reset. The PRBS checker is held in reset while `~rx_done || ~tx_done`. **`error` goes only to the VIO** (`probe_in12`, `probe_in13`), nothing resets on it. `rxbufstatus` goes to a VIO probe; `txbufstatus` is brought out and unused.
**Controller internals** (`gtwiz_buffbypass_tx/rx.v`, cleartext in the IP): start -> pulse `TXDLYSRESET` for one clock -> wait for a rising edge of the master's `TXSYNCDONE` -> `done` high and `error <= ~TXPHALIGNDONE` for that clock. `done` and `error` clear **only when the next start begins** (the stale-done window that `gt_bp_gate` already handles; the AMD example has no gate for it; its timers hide it).

**Compared with ours** (`rm_gp_bypass/rtl/b96_core.sv` and `gt_bp_gate.sv`):
- Reset input: AMD's is the *synchronised* inverse of the user-clock-active; ours is the unsynchronised `!utx_act_t`/`!urx_act_r`. The active signals come from the Wizard's user-clock helper in the same domain, so this is probably safe; the build's `report_cdc` shows 0 critical. **Difference noted, not an error;** matching AMD (a reset synchroniser) is cheap if the board shows an alignment hang. **UNCONFIRMED:** whether the unsynchronised release can start the controller mid-clock-enable.
- The RX controller's extra "wait for TX done" term is **loopback-specific** in AMD's comment; we do not wait for it. Correct for a real link (RX alignment does not depend on our TX).
- Error: AMD only displays it; ours (`gt_bp_gate`) holds the direction in reset until the next GT reset. Stricter. Keep, and record `error` in the host bench.
- Timeouts: AMD's example has a 30 ms and a 130 ms retry. Ours retries only on a full GT restart by the driver. **Worth adding to the board plan:** a timeout-and-retry check (stage 1 already restarts 100 times).

## 3. TXBUFSTATUS and RXBUFSTATUS with the buffers bypassed (UG576)

**UG576 says nothing about their meaning in bypass.** Table 3-18 (p.136): `TXBUFSTATUS[1]` TX FIFO overflow or underflow (sticky until the buffer reset), `[0]` FIFO at least half full; the port also carries the TX asynchronous gearbox FIFO status (p.130). Table 4-33 (p.271) and 4-36 (p.277): `RXBUFSTATUS[2:0]` elastic-buffer fill (000 nominal, 001/010 below/above the clock-correction limits, 101/110 underflow/overflow; not sticky); and `[1:0]` the RX gearbox FIFO (Table 4-44). **No table states a value in bypass.** With `TXBUF_EN = FALSE` and `RXBUF_EN = FALSE` there is no FIFO to report, so the codes are undefined: UNCONFIRMED, not read. The AMD example brings them to a VIO and takes no action; the TX-bypass section never mentions `TXBUFSTATUS` (p.137 to 142).
**Read instead on the host bench:** the controller outputs `gtwiz_buffbypass_{tx,rx}_done_out` and `_error_out` (the `GT_BUF` bits 15:10 added by 96F1 already carry them); `TXPHALIGNDONE`/`RXPHALIGNDONE` are consumed inside the core and are **not** ports in this configuration (96F1), so error (= PHALIGNDONE low at completion) is the only view of them; `RXCDRLOCK`; block lock; the design's own counters. The delay-aligner tap is not visible without extra ports (`RXPH_MONITOR_SEL`/`DMONITOR` per the third-party CERN slides; UNCONFIRMED for this configuration).

## 4. TXPI with the TX buffer bypassed, and as a loop-timing actuator

What UG576 says: the TXPI ppm controller moves the PI that sits between the QPLL and the PMA parallel clock (Figure 3-29 text, p.159 to 160; Table 3-33 p.164 to 166); `TXPIPPMPD` powers the block down (p.164); `TXPHDLYPD` is 0 in TX bypass (p.85). **UG576 never says TXPI may not be used with TX bypass, and never says how they interact.** The only statement connecting the two: `TX_PROGCLK_SEL = POSTPI` "can be used to generate a parallel clock with a certain ppm offset created by the TX PI", `PREPI` "for applications where minimal or fixed latency is needed" (p.160); and with the PI active, `TXPIPPMEN`/`TXPIPPMSEL` must be 0 for far-end PMA loopback (p.89).

What the generated cores show (Vivado 2026.1, this brief and 96F1, `results/b96f1/wizard_probe/`, `results/b96f2/`):
- Buffers on (today): `TXOUTCLKSEL = 010` (TXOUTCLKPMA, the post-PI clock), `txpippmpd_in = 0`.
- Both buffers bypassed: `TXOUTCLKSEL = 101` (TXPROGDIVCLK), **`txpippmpd_in` tied to 1'b1 and `TXPIPPMPD_VAL = 1` (PPM controller powered down)**, `TX_PROGCLK_SEL = PREPI` in **both** (netlist property, `netlist_attrs_*.full.txt` line 494). **The Wizard therefore both powers the TXPI ppm block down and takes the user clock before the PI** in a bypass core: a TXPI offset would move the serial output and `XCLK` but **not** `TXUSRCLK`. The two clocks would then drift apart at the offset rate, which the TX phase and delay alignment (range +-4 ns, "continues to adjust `TXUSRCLK` … for temperature and voltage", p.142) was not described to follow. Not stated in UG576; **UNCONFIRMED whether it would hold, slip or fail.**
- Third-party evidence (CERN FDF 2025, 2026, ZCU102 GTH, slides): the PI was moved by hand through the DRP "to align TXOUTCLK" with `TXOUTCLK = TXOUTCLKPMA`, and a PICXO/FRACXO core "modulat[ed] the PI"; the 2026 talk uses buffer bypass for the data path and the PI for the TX frame clock phase with a fabric FIFO. That is a GTH with PI motion and bypass, **with the user clock taken after the PI (`TXOUTCLKPMA`)**. UG576's use-mode list (p.141) names only `TXOUTCLKSEL` 011, 100 and 101 for TX bypass; the CERN set-up deviates from it. Secondary, one group, not reproduced here.

**Verdict.** (a) In 96F1's bypass build the ppm registers do nothing: confirmed, by two sources (wrapper attribute and port tie). (b) TXPI together with TX bypass and a pre-PI user clock: **not documented, UNCONFIRMED, treat as not usable** until a board probe shows alignment surviving a PI step. (c) With a post-PI user clock (`TXOUTCLKSEL = 010`, `TX_PROGCLK_SEL = POSTPI`) the combination exists on a GTH in third-party use, but the Wizard does not generate it and the in-core Wizard offers no way to set these; it needs the hand-instantiated channel too. (d) For loop timing the QPLL fractional-N is AMD's actuator (XAPP1276), with the TXPI left powered down. **96F's "two windows biased by TXPI" idea loses its hardware basis**; the one-sided windows with a QPLL reset at each hop remain, or a centred reference (not available, section 7).

## 5. The QPLL outside the Wizard core (`LOCATE_COMMON = EXAMPLE_DESIGN`)

Core port changes (96F1, `ports_diff_common_outside_vs_inside.txt`, confirmed by this brief's generated example): the core loses `gtrefclk00_in`, `qpll0outclk_out`, `qpll0outrefclk_out` and gains `qpll0clk_in`, `qpll0refclk_in`, `qpll1clk_in`, `qpll1refclk_in`, `gtwiz_reset_qpll0lock_in`, `gtwiz_reset_qpll0reset_out`.
What AMD's example wrapper does around the COMMON: instantiates a generated `*_gthe4_common_wrapper` (UNISIM `GTHE4_COMMON` with the same attributes the in-core one has: `QPLL0_FBDIV` 66, `QPLL0_SDM_CFG0` 0x0080, the SDM ports **not tied** inside the wrapper); gives it `GTREFCLK00` from the reference input, `QPLL0REFCLKSEL` 001, `QPLL0LOCKEN` 1, `QPLL0RESET` from the core's `gtwiz_reset_qpll0reset_out`; feeds `QPLL0LOCK` back to `gtwiz_reset_qpll0lock_in`; and ties **`SDM0DATA`, `SDM0RESET`, `SDM0TOGGLE`, `SDM0WIDTH` to 0**: the example is the hook, the dynamic use is ours. The generated `.xdc` is **identical** for the in-core and the outside variants (pins, `create_clock` on the reference and the free-running clock, synchroniser false paths): **no constraint change** from moving the QPLL. What changes is only RTL wiring: our top must hold the `GTHE4_COMMON`, take the lock into the core's reset controller, and drive `SDM0*`.
Run-time driving of the SDM: UG576 p.57 to 58 (Figures 2-14, 2-15, Table 2-16: `SDM*DATA` stable one system-clock cycle before `SDM*TOGGLE`, toggle high 3 and low 3 `FBCLK` cycles, data frozen throughout; `USRCLK` or `DRPCLK` may be the system clock) and the 200 ppm rule after a QPLL reset (p.58). XAPP1276 is the only AMD example found; it drives `SDM_DATA_O[24:0]` and `SDM_TOGGLE_O` from its DPLL (Table 1). **A contradiction in UG576:** Table 2-14 (p.54) calls `SDM0TOGGLE` "Reserved. Set to 1'b0." and `QPLL0FBDIV` reserved, while p.57 to 58 use `SDM*TOGGLE`; XAPP1276 uses it (UltraScale+ only). Treat the Table 2-14 text as stale. No AMD answer record for dynamic SDM was found (the AR search tools are not reachable). Wizard width: `SDM0WIDTH` "00" = 24 bits (Table 2-14); the fraction is `SDMDATA / 2^24` and unsigned (p.51, Equation 2-6), so at N = 66.0 the fractional part can only add.

## 6. AR 68177 and AR 69011: GTH buffer latency

**No GTH figure was obtained.** UG576 (p.18) lists "UltraScale GTH: TX and RX Latency Values" (AR 64309) and "UltraScale+ GTH: TX and RX Latency Values" (AR 68177); both pages are unreachable from here. AR 69011 is **a GTY UltraScale+ record** (the blog and PG210 name it so). What is available is the blog's calculation for **a GTY with both buffers bypassed** (section 1): TX 141 UI plus RX 133 UI at 32-bit width, i.e. about 13.7 ns plus 12.9 ns; at 20-bit 89 plus 93 UI. These are a GTY and a different datapath width from ours (64-bit user, 32-bit internal; blog: 32-bit user). The record's "default (buffer on)" figures that 96F used (TX 7.7 to 10.9 ns, RX 13.2 to 17.1 ns) remain third-party GTY numbers. So **96F1's prediction still rests on a GTY figure**: UNCONFIRMED for the K26's GTH. Pointers if the owner wants it settled: AR 68177 and AR 64309 through a logged-in browser, or a board measurement (the stage 1 test already measures E).

## 7. What this changes in 96F's plan

1. **V1 is redefined and becomes plausible.** Replace "TXUSRCLK2 and RXUSRCLK2 are the same net, the recovered clock" by V1': both user clocks from the transmit `TXOUTCLK` (`BUFG_GT`), the QPLL pulled onto the recovered rate by a fabric DPLL, RX in bypass with `RXSYNC_SKIP_DA = 1`. AMD built and measured exactly this on a GTY; XAPP1276 supplies the controller. **New risks V1' adds:** (a) the RX bypass alignment requires `XCLK` and `RXUSRCLK` at the same frequency (UG576 p.255), true only **after** the loop is locked, so the RX alignment must be (re)run after lock (the blog does not state its order: UNCONFIRMED); (b) skipped delay alignment means no temperature compensation on RX: the DPLL's phase hold is the only thing keeping the recovered phase, so a fabric clock phase fault is a data fault; (c) V2 (matched clocks, RX on `RXOUTCLK`) is unchanged and is still the lower-risk first step; V1' is what moves the crossing out of the fabric.
2. **Recommended order unchanged but with a new gate:** B1, then V2 behind the loop, then V1' (documented for multi-lane GTH, tested on a GTY single-lane; its single-lane GTH sequence is the thing to prove on the board).
3. **The loop's actuator is the QPLL fractional-N only.** TXPI is powered down by the Wizard in bypass and is off the table for the two-window scheme (section 4). Window hops (QPLL reset) are therefore needed at a one-sided 156.25 MHz reference, **as 96F found**. AMD's own recipe avoids this with a programmable reference (Si570): the KR260 XO is fixed (96F addendum). Re-state the options: one-sided windows with hops (chatter at near-zero offset, which AMD avoids), or a centred reference (not on this board).
4. **Loop parameters** (96F section 6): keep the model; add AMD's numbers as cross-checks (bandwidth below 1 kHz, comparison frequency 2 MHz or less, `G2` larger than `G1`, HOLD, lock-wide then narrow). No change to the pass criteria.
5. **Bypass controller drive** (96F1 reset UNCONFIRMED): now checked: TX the same intent, our reset is unsynchronised, RX needs no TX term on a real link, add a 30 ms and 130 ms timeout-and-retry check to stage 1 for the case where alignment never completes. Status inputs: use done and error, not `TXBUFSTATUS`/`RXBUFSTATUS`.
6. **Board test plan**: Stage 0 is done. Add to stage 1: confirm `error` stays 0 over the 100 restarts and record the time from reset release to `done`. Add to stage 2: a V1' sequencing step (lock, then RX realign) before the direct crossing. Stage 3 unchanged. The TXPI-in-bypass question is a probe for a **separate** build (post-PI user clock) and is not in the plan.
7. **Latency prediction** is unchanged (still GTY-based). Not corrected: only a board measurement settles it.

## 8. Still unknown after this brief

1. XAPP1276 in English and the reference design (a members' download). 2. AR 68177 and AR 64309 (GTH latencies). 3. Whether a single-lane GTH accepts `RXSYNC_SKIP_DA = 1` with `RXUSRCLK` shared from the TX clock and aligns after the loop locks. 4. `TXBUFSTATUS`/`RXBUFSTATUS` codes in bypass (UG576 silent). 5. TXPI step behaviour under bypass with a pre-PI user clock. 6. How the DPLL's phase detector input (`RXOUTCLK` of the bypassed RX) behaves across an RX realignment. 7.
