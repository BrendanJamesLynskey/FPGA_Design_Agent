# The KR260's PL Ethernet PHY: TI DP83867 (what b58a relies on)

Brief 175, 2026-11-22. Before this brief the design and its notes assumed a Marvell 88E1111. AMD's carrier BOM (XTP757, row 60:
DP83867CSRGZT, four parts: U36, U79, U80, U81) and the carrier schematic (XTP743, sheets 18 to 21, `DP83867CSRGZ`) say the PHYs are
TI DP83867. (AMD's Vivado board file `kr260_carrier` still lists a Marvell M88E1111 for `pl_gem2`/`pl_gem3`; on this evidence it is wrong
for this carrier revision, and the schematic wins.) The design has never run on a board.

Sources, cited and not copied:

- **Datasheet:** TI DP83867IR/DP83867CR, document SNLS484J, February 2015, revised June 2026 (`https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/dp83867cr`,
  read 2026-11-22). Section and table numbers below are that revision's.
- **Schematic:** AMD XTP743, `038-05101-01_sck-kr_reva02_SKIT_20211015.pdf`, sheets 20 (PL GEM2, U79) and 21 (PL GEM3, U80). AMD files
  are private (`_handoff/fpga_agent/docs/kr260/`); only facts are quoted here.
- **BOM:** AMD XTP757.

## Which PHY is which port

| Port | Connector | PHY | Sheet | Interface pins | MDIO address (schematic note "ADDR") | Strap network |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (trading) | J10B, Ethernet 2 | U79 | 20 | HPA | 0x02 | RX_D0 pin: 5.76 kohm up + 2.49 kohm down = 4-level strap mode 3 (PHY_ADD1:0 = 10); RX_CTRL: mode 3 (autoneg-disable = 0) |
| B (generator) | J10A, Ethernet 3 | U80 | 21 | HPB | 0x03 | RX_D0 pin: 2.49 kohm up, nothing down = mode 4 (PHY_ADD1:0 = 11); RX_CTRL: mode 3 |

Checked against the datasheet (section 7.5.1, table 7-6): the 48-pin RGZ part has 16 addresses (0 to 15), PHY_ADD[3:0] come from the
RX_D0 and RX_D2 pins, an unstrapped pin reads as mode 1 (00); so U79 = 0b0010 = 2 and U80 = 0b0011 = 3, as the schematic notes. The two
PHYs have **separate MDIO/MDC pairs** (HPA03 and HPB03), each with a 2.21 kohm pull-up to 1.8 V on the sheet, so there is no shared bus;
the host reads port A at address 2 and port B at address 3 (the earlier plan scanned all 32 addresses; an RGZ part only answers 0 to 15).
`mdio_master.sv` is a generic clause 22 master (PHY address 5 bits, register 5 bits), so no RTL change is needed for the addresses.

## RGMII mode and the internal clock delays

- **RGMII is the default mode on this package:** RGMIICTL (0x0032) bit 7, RGMII_EN, resets to 1 on the RGZ part (table 8-33). The RX_D6
  "RGMII disable" strap is a PAP (64-pin) feature only.
- **Both internal delays are on by default** (RGMIICTL bit 1 = TX clock delay, bit 0 = RX clock delay, both reset to 1, table 8-33). The
  delay *amount* is RGMIIDCTL (0x0086): bits 7:4 TX, 3:0 RX, 0.25 ns steps from 0.25 to 4.0 ns (table 8-45). The reset value of each
  field's upper three bits comes from the straps (LED_2, LED_1 for TX; GPIO_0, GPIO_1 for RX, table 7-6 and 7-9, 7-10). On this carrier
  every one of those strap resistor positions is **do-not-populate**, so every strap reads mode 1, which tables 7-9 and 7-10 give as
  **2.0 ns for both TX and RX**. (The datasheet's register table and strap table do not agree on the encoding of the reset value: a
  strap of 000 is "2.0 ns" in the strap tables, but the RGMIIDCTL table reads 0x0 as 0.25 ns. Revision J changed the field default from
  0111 to "[7:5] strap, [4] 0". The board plan therefore reads STRAP_STS2 (0x006F) and RGMIIDCTL back before relying on either.)
- **Reaching these registers:** 0x0032, 0x0086 and 0x006F are extended registers. Clause 22 access goes through REGCR (0x0D) and ADDAR
  (0x0E): write 0x001F to REGCR, the register address to ADDAR, 0x401F to REGCR, then read or write the data through ADDAR (section 7.4.2.1,
  datasheet text). Each extended access is four MDIO writes (plus one read for a read).
- **Receive (PHY to FPGA):** with its RX delay on, the PHY outputs RX_CLK centred on the data. Datasheet table 6.9 gives data valid at least
  1.2 ns before and 1.2 ns after the edge ("Data to Clock output Setup/Hold (at Transmitter, internal delay)"), the same numbers as the
  Marvell-based draft, so the RX constraints (`-max 2.8`, `-min 1.2`) and the 1000 ps IDELAY do not change.
- **Transmit (FPGA to PHY):** the PHY delays GTX_CLK by D_TX. The datasheet's receiver figure is "data to clock input skew, 1.0 min, 1.8 nom,
  2.6 max ns" with a note that, without the internal delay, the board must add more than 1.5 ns to the clock; it gives no separate
  pin-level data-valid window for the internal-delay case. **Reading adopted here** (an interpretation, to be confirmed by the board's
  delay sweep): the 1.0 to 2.6 ns applies to the total clock-after-data at the PHY's sampling point, D_TX plus the pin-level offset.
  With D_TX = 1.75 ns a data change may come up to 0.75 ns after its TXC edge and no earlier than 0.85 ns before it. D_TX = 2.0 ns (the strap
  default) gives 1.0 after and 0.6 before; the RGMII v2.0 generic +/-0.5 ns is narrower still. The Marvell-based draft's 0.9 ns / 1.3 ns window
  does not apply to this PHY.

## Why the transmit window had to change, and what the FPGA does

The FPGA side stays as it was: data and TXC leave edge-aligned from ODDRE1 on one clock, the PHY adds the delay (the strap-default PHY
behaviour, so nothing about the FPGA "no longer should"). What changed is the numbers: `design.xdc` and `build.yaml` carry the DP83867
window above, with a board skew allowance taken from the carrier copper (brief 162's Allegro dump: PHY2 nets 7.07 to 7.34 in, PHY3 nets
2.34 to 2.87 in; the spread within a group, 0.27 in and 0.53 in, at 150 to 190 ps/in gives at most 0.05 ns and 0.09 ns). The SOM's routing is
not in AMD's files and is not counted. The PHY-side change is one register write per port: RGMIIDCTL[7:4] = 0110 (1.75 ns), RX field
unchanged (docs/board_run_plan.md step 5). See `results/175_b58a_dp83867/` for the build that checks it.

## Reset and start-up timing (datasheet sections 6.6, 6.7, 7.5.5)

| Item | Datasheet | What the design does |
|---|---|---|
| Reset pulse width | 1 us minimum (X1 clock stable for it) | RESET_B held low for 2^21 clocks = 16.8 ms after the MMCM locks |
| After reset, before MDC carries a management frame | MDIO high for 32-bit preamble after 195 us | the host's first MDIO access is a JTAG-scale time later (milliseconds) |
| Hardware strap latch | 120 ns after reset release | straps are resistor-set (no FPGA drive on those pins) |
| After power-up, before management | 200 ms | the board is powered and idle before the PL is loaded (board_run_plan.md section 1) |
| RESET_B on the carrier | n/a | 10 kohm pull-up to 1.8 V (R376/R401): the PHY is out of reset before the PL is configured |
| X1 reference | 25 MHz | the same 25 MHz oscillator (SiT1602 U92 through buffer U91, schematic sheet 16) feeds both PHYs and the FPGA's HPA clock input |
| MDC frequency | 2.5 MHz nominal, 25 MHz max | `MDIO_DIV` 32 at 125 MHz = 1.95 MHz |

A software reset that matters: **BMCR bit 15 resets only the IEEE registers**; the RGMII registers 0x0032 and 0x0086 are extended and
survive it. The global software reset (CTRL 0x001F bit 15) resets *all* registers to their defaults, so it would undo a RGMIIDCTL write; the
board plan does not use it after the write.

## Latency the PHY adds (for the honest tick-to-trade)

Datasheet table 6.9 lists, for 1000BASE-T, "RGMII to MDI latency" 88 ns (TTXLAT) and "MDI to RGMII latency" 288 ns (TRXLAT); the features list
says "TX < 90 ns, RX < 290 ns". The text extract of the table does not show whether 88 and 288 sit in its typical or maximum column, and the
datasheet does not name the exact start and end events (which bit at the RGMII pins, which symbol on the wire). Treat them as figures
to within the datasheet's rounding, not as measurements. For the trading port (a packet in on port A, a response out on port A) the PHY
adds about 288 ns on the way in and about 88 ns on the way out, 376 ns together, **in addition to every FPGA figure in this design's
README, which starts at the RX start-of-frame inside the FPGA and ends at the first response byte leaving it**. The cable, the magnetics and
the link partner's own PHY are further additions. In 1000 Mb/s, the receive start-of-frame can additionally vary by -8 to +8 ns from one
link-up to the next (table 6.16); the datasheet gives a procedure (set Sync FIFO Control 0x00E9 to 0xDF22 before link-up, read Skew FIFO
Status 0x0055, section 7.3.2.1) to measure and remove it. Packet-to-packet the variation is constant while the link stays up. Neither the
figures nor the procedure have been used on the board.

## What is not known

- The pin-level setup/hold the PHY needs at its GTX_CLK/TXD inputs with its delay at a value other than 2.0 ns: only the 1.0 to 2.6 ns
  window above is given. The board's delay sweep (`board_run_plan.md` step 5b) measures the working range.
- The SOM's routing skew between TXC and TXD.
- Whether 88/288 ns are typical or maximum values, and their start/end events.
- The AMD board file's Marvell entry (the file says M88E1111; the BOM and schematic say DP83867): the board file is not changed here.
