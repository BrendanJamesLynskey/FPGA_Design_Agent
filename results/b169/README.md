# results/b169: where the remaining tick-to-trade clocks go, and two prototype cuts (brief 169: simulation, a Vivado build of the cut design, no board)

Branch `b169-next-cuts`. The fastest build so far is `lat_all3_bypass` (brief 126). Its board run A (2026-11-22, stamp 9641284d) measured L - E = 24 clocks (148.1 ns net of fibre; per-link mean 23.77 clocks). This folder says where those clocks go, lists the cuts that might remove more, prototypes two of them behind parameters (default off) in `designs/b96_kr260_10g/lat_cuts169/`, and pre-registers a board test.

## Definitions (the owner's rule: every figure says what it is)
Clocks are 6.2058 ns (161.1328125 MHz), counted in xsim (zero-latency transceiver stand-in, so no GT, no optics) or from the FPGA's own 32-bit counter on the board (one-clock resolution, plus or minus 1; not at the pins; not STAC-T0; see `docs/latency_definitions.md`).
- **Simulated tick-to-trade (T2T):** start event = the GT RXDATA word that holds the last bit of the block carrying the price (word 7 of the frame, bytes 56 to 63; price bytes 54 to 57 are the high half in word 6 and the low half in word 7) is captured; end event = the LAST bit of the order's start block is on GT TXDATA (the convention of 96E; the FIRST bit is one clock earlier). Path between: PCS receive, MAC alignment, decision, the receive-to-transmit crossing, order start, transmit MAC, transmit PCS.
- **Board L - E = E + D:** E = TX start stamp of a probe frame to the RX start stamp of the same frame (one pass: transmit MAC, PCS, GT, module, fibre, GT, PCS, MAC, crossing); D (register `d_last`) = RX start stamp of the feed frame to the TX start stamp of the order. L - E is therefore first bit in to first bit out at the transceivers, optics and 0.83 ns of fibre included, FPGA counters. It starts at the frame's first bit, so it includes the 8.5 clocks (52.8 ns, derived in 109 from the bench frame layout) it takes the price to arrive; simulated T2T does not.
- **Stages that add 0 clocks** add nothing because they are the capture edge itself (stage A), or are combinational in the clock of the previous register (the decision `fire`).

Predicted saving in clocks of the order's start (simulated, except where marked); risk in timing at 161.13 MHz (TXUSRCLK2 and RXUSRCLK2) and 156.25 MHz (reference), CDC, protocol, AMD recommendations; whether STRICT (decision 31: an order leaves only after its packet's check passes; here, an order frame is built speculatively and made bad (poisoned FCS) unless its feed frame's verdict is known good when its last word is built) still holds.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **EARLY_MAC (prototyped):** decide on the MAC's aligned word before its output register | 1.00 (33 orders, two phases) | Timing: `x_d` register, realign mux, 32-bit compare, toggle flop in one clock (see Timing). CDC none new. ## The two prototypes (`designs/b96_kr260_10g/lat_cuts169/rtl/`, default parameters 0)
Nothing under `rtl/`, `lat_all3/` or `rm_gp_bypass/` was edited (a changed source would change the build stamp of `lat_all3_bypass`, whose stamp 9641284d the board run recorded). Seven files are copies with the brief's lines added: `mac64_rx.sv`, `trade64.sv` (from `lat_all3`), `rx_path_legacy.sv` (EARLY); `mac64_tx.sv`, `pcs_tx.sv`, `frame_src.sv`, `tx_path.sv` (from `lat_all3`) (FAST_PRE); `b96_core.sv` (from `rm_gp_bypass`: two new parameters passed through). `test_169_cuts.py::test_defaults_are_off_and_files_are_copies` checks the defaults and that each copy keeps all but a few lines of its parent. With both parameters 0 the xsim results equal brief 126's `lat_all3_bypass` exactly (T2T 8.0, 7.75, 7.5, 7.25 at the four phases; mean 7.62; strobe 5.62; D 7.37).

**EARLY_MAC.** `mac64_rx` also outputs `e_v/e_d/e_sof`, the combinational D inputs of its `m_v/m_d/m_sof` registers (a simulation check prints `EARLY MISMATCH` if a register ever differs from the early word one clock before; none in any run). `trade64` with `EARLY = 1` runs its word counter, header checks, price compare and `fire` on that stream. The order-content check, `cls_*` pulses and the verdict use the same stream; only `m_end`, `m_fcs_ok`, `m_len`, `m_err` stay registered, and they arrive at least as long after the last word as before. A frame's `m_end` is three clocks after its terminate; the next frame's first word can be two clocks after it, so with EARLY a new frame's word 0 may be processed one clock before the previous frame's `m_end`: nothing in `trade64` depends on that order (`in_frame` is not read; `fired_this` and `verdict_*` are per decision), and the bench's back-to-back and cut-frame tests pass.

**FAST_PRE.** When the frame source starts an order while the MAC is idle and the gearbox is not paused, `mac64_tx` raises `pre_now` (combinational): `pcs_tx` sends the start block this clock, chosen at the registers' inputs between two values both computed from registers only (the normal block and the constant start block, scrambled with the current history and gearboxed at the current fill), so `go_p` adds only a select; the MAC's own output register takes word 0 at the same edge (word 0 is static configuration), `frame_src` starts at word 1, and `sop` is high in the go clock (so the board's D stamp moves with the saving). Otherwise (a pause, a busy MAC) the base path runs. **Live-field bus margin:** `frame_src` samples the order fields from the quasi-static bus until word 3 is loaded and freezes them; with FAST_PRE the frame is one clock earlier, so it freezes the sample taken at c3 instead of c4 (c0 = the go clock). The bus is three synchroniser flops behind its source, `go_p` two, so the bus is valid from about c1; the margin falls from 3 clocks to 2. The check of the sampled sequence (`stale`) moves from word 3 to word 4 for such frames so it judges the sample the frame carries. Xsim shows `stale` 0 and `order_ok` = orders at every phase (the clocks are phase-locked in the stand-in; the board's phase is per lock: this is why the pre-registration treats `stale` as the FAIL detector).

## Why D came out 8 rather than 6 (board run A of `lat_all3_bypass`, 2026-11-22)
**What the board gave** (`board_per_link.txt`, 100 restarts, from `stage3.json`): E 16.0 (per-link histogram 15: 19, 16: 71, 17: 10; mean 15.91), L 40.0 (39: 44, 40: 56; mean 39.56), D by the stage3 summary 8.0 (= 40 - 2 x 16), but the register D (`d_last`, read per link) is 7 on 29 links, 8 on 56, 9 on 15 (mean 7.86). L - E = E + D: median-based 24, mean 23.77 clocks (148.1 ns net of fibre by the bench's rule).

**1. Part of the "miss" is how D was predicted (settled by the records).** Brief 126 predicted D = 6 by taking 97C's `lat_all3` as D = L - 2E from medians: 46 - 2 x 20 = 6. The same 97C run's register D averaged **7.19** (81 links 7, 19 links 8), and its per-link L - 2E averaged 7.06. A median of L minus twice a median of E is biased low by about a clock; the per-link quantities are not. Like for like, the bypass moved D from 7.19 to 7.86 (**+0.67 clock**), not from 6 to 8; E fell 3.76 (19.67 to 15.91); so L - E fell **3.09** (26.86 to 23.77), against the 4.0 predicted from E alone. The headline for every later pre-registration is therefore the per-link mean of L - E, with the median-based figure beside it.

**2. What simulation says.** The simulated D (receive start strobe to order start) is 7.37 clocks (7.27 with 33 orders) for both `all3` and `all3_bypass`, equal to the board's register D for `all3` (7.19) within 0.2 clock, and does not change with the bypass (the stand-in has no buffers, and none of the logic that sets D differs). It is flat over 16 phases of the receive clock (`sweep33/base`: 7.27 at every phase) while the receive start strobe S (what E is made of) falls by one clock across the same phases (6.0 to 5.0). So the model predicts D independent of the clock phase, and S, hence E, varying by about one clock with it.

**3. What the board shows that the model does not.** With the bypass, E spreads over 3 values (15 to 17) instead of 2 (19, 20) and D over 3 (7 to 9) instead of 2 (7, 8); and D is anti-correlated with E: links with E = 15 have mean D 8.37, E = 16: 7.80, E = 17: 7.30 (with the buffers on, E = 19: 7.12, E = 20: 7.22, no relation). E + D still varies a little (23.4, 23.8, 24.3).

**4. Candidate causes**, in the order the evidence favours them:
 - **A. A different phase threshold in the go crossing than in the start-strobe crossing, with a per-lock phase that the bypass spreads over the whole clock.** In the zero-delay model every crossing samples at the same phase, so D is flat. A real crossing has its own route and clock delay between the toggle flop and its first synchroniser flop; if the go crossing's is longer than the start-strobe crossing's, there is a band of phases in which the go pulse takes one clock more than the strobe, D reads one more and E does not move the same way. **Simulation experiment** (`skew_sim.sh`, `skew/`): the go crossing replaced by a behavioural copy (it reproduces the XPM model at 0 ns delay: T2T 8.0 at phase 0, 7.23 at 4800 ps) with 2.2 ns between its toggle flop and first synchroniser flop: D stays 7.37 up to 4000 ps and becomes **8.37 from 4800 ps**, while S keeps falling (5.36 at 4000 ps, 5.23 at 4800 ps): a smaller E with a larger D, the board's pattern. With the buffers on, the lock phase may be confined to a narrower range (E has only two values), which would keep most links outside the band; the bypass alignment sets the phase anew at every lock. The 2.2 ns is an assumed delay, not a measurement, and 9 on the board (15 links) is not reproduced by one band.
 - **B. The bypass changed the distribution of the per-lock phase** (the same threshold effect as A with a different spread). Not testable from the records: nothing in the bench reads the phase.
 - **C. Quantisation of the minima.** E, L are per-link minima over a few frames at one-clock resolution; the register D is a last value. This explains at most 0.5 clock of the 0.67 and nothing about the 9s; it fully explains the 6-versus-8 prediction miss (point 1).
 - **D. A real extra clock in the bypass build's logic or placement** (for instance the go crossing placed further from the transmit logic). Simulation cannot show it; Vivado's route delays on the crossing nets can.

**5. Cheap tests that tell these apart** (none needs new RTL):
 1. *Free, done:* the per-link tables above (the anti-correlation points at A or B, against D).
 2. *Minutes, one light Vivado session on an existing checkpoint (no build):* open brief 126's routed checkpoint and report the net delay from the toggle flop to the first synchroniser flop of `u_p_go` against `u_p_so`, `u_p_fe` and `u_p_or` (`report_timing -from ... -to ...` shows data path delay and clock skew). If the go crossing's is 1 to 2 ns longer than the strobe's, cause A is supported quantitatively (rerun `skew_sim.sh` at the measured value); if equal, A falls and D is a candidate. The brief's rules put Vivado under a heavy slot, so this was not run here (both slots were held by other sessions for the whole run).
 3. *Board, same bench, no hardware change:* run the buffers-on (`lat_all3`) and bypass bitstreams in one session and compare the per-link (E, D) pairs; A and B predict the anti-correlation only with the bypass; repeat the bypass over 3 power cycles: if the pairs' distribution is stable, the phase distribution is a property of the bypass alignment (B), if it changes between power-ups, it is the lock phase (A).
 4. *If A is confirmed and one clock matters:* give the go crossing and the strobe crossings the same structure (both `DEST_SYNC_FF 2`, no output register, placed together), or remove the crossing (cut C6).

## Known gaps and limits
- **Nothing ran on a board.** Every saving is xsim with a zero-latency transceiver stand-in and phase-locked-per-run clocks; the board's per-lock phase moves the crossing by up to a clock and, as run A showed, D by a clock more than the model says (section "Why D came out 8"). The prediction is arithmetic on run A's per-link means and the simulated savings.
- The D hypothesis (A) rests on one simulated delay (2.2 ns) that is an assumption; the routed delays were not read (test 2 not run: no heavy slot was free).
- The bypass start-up scenarios (hung alignment, retries) were not re-run for the cuts; no reset or controller logic was touched.
- Residual of the board budget (8.64 clocks) is a subtraction and carries 109's 2 to 3 clock accounting difference; it is not a measurement of the GT.
- C3 (decide from stage A) and C4 (price-split early fire) were not prototyped; C5 (book overlap, ITCH path) was not evaluated. `lat_cuts169` is built with both cuts on only; a WARN or FAIL on the board would need the two single-cut builds to attribute.
- The bench's `core_cocotb_default.py` cannot run the bypass core (no Icarus model of `xpm_cdc_async_rst`); the combinations ran on the base core with the two parameters patched in, and the bypass core's behaviour is covered by the xsim runs with real XPM macros.
- Citations for UG949, UG974, UG576, AR 68177 and IEEE 802.3 clause 49 are as recorded in this repository's own documents (design.md, brief 126 and 109); they were not re-read for this brief.

## Gotchas
1. `core_cocotb_default.py` injected its two poison tests by `trade64`'s internal word index, and two tests looked for the order's start by `cur_kind`. With EARLY_MAC (index one clock ahead) and FAST_PRE (start stamp before `cur_kind` loads) those probes moved: the injection landed on a different bit of a different 66-bit block (header instead of payload) and the tests failed, which was the test's coupling, not the design's. The bench now rebuilds the word index from the registered MAC word and uses `bus_kind` (equal to `cur_kind` whenever `sop` is registered); with the base RTL every result is unchanged (16 of 16).
2. A median of L minus twice a median of E is not D (see "Why D came out 8"); quote per-link means and L - E.
3. The testbench `tb_lat_core.sv` detects "word 7 at the MAC" by `m_v` and `td_wi`; with EARLY_MAC it must wait for `td_wi == 8` (`make_tb169.py`).
4. In `core_sim.sh` the top module keeps brief 126's derived name `tb_lat_core126`.
5. A delay inside a simulation of an XPM macro's input does nothing (the first attempt delayed `r_fire` before the toggle flop: no effect). The experiment needs the delay between the toggle flop and the first synchroniser flop, so `skew_sim.sh` replaces the go crossing by a behavioural copy and first checks that it reproduces the XPM numbers.
