# results/33_vivado_speed_2026_1: brief 33's build-speed measurements on Vivado 2026.1 (brief 49)

Brief 33's thread-count and build-cache measurements repeated on **Vivado 2026.1** (Basic licence tier), 2026-11-12 02:47 to 05:17,
on the build PC, one build at a time in a heavy slot, every build under a **6 GB** cap (brief 33 used 8 GB, and 12 GB for the rig).
Other sessions' Vivado and simulation jobs ran on the PC that night: `measure.sh` waits for any other Vivado before each build
(`waited ... s` lines in `batch.log`), but a job that started during a build shared the CPU with it, so the wall times carry that load.
**The numbers are in [results.md](results.md)** (and `summary.json`), generated by `summarise.py` from `raw/<label>/`.

    python3 benchmark/tools/ref_build.py benchmark/course/tasks/c10_dds_sine --stage-only      # and c13_nexys_harris_vga
    results/33_vivado_speed_2026_1/measure.sh results/33_vivado_speed_2026_1/jobs_2026_1.txt
    UNIT=b49-licence-check results/33_vivado_speed_2026_1/licence_check.sh
    python3 results/33_vivado_speed_2026_1/summarise.py

Results:

- **Thread count:** 1, 2 and 4 threads gave the same placement hash, routing hash and WNS on the small (c10) and mid (c13) designs,
  as on the earlier release. c13: 546 s at 1 thread (mean of two) against 413 s at 4 (24 % less). c10: no gain measurable under that
  night's load (250 s at 1 thread, 257 s at 4, mean of two each). Peak anonymous memory about 2.1 GB (c10) and 2.3 GB (c13) at every
  count.
- **The CDC rig (36k LUT) at 1 thread:** 1,748 s (29 minutes), peak anonymous memory 3,870 MiB, WNS +0.232 ns. **Not run at 2 and 4
  threads:** on the earlier release they peaked at 5.1 and 8.2 GB, at or above today's 6 GB cap; those figures are withdrawn.
- **Build cache:** one agent run's build sequence took 304.6 s instead of a modelled 1,266.9 s on c10 (76 % saved) and 439.5 s
  instead of 2,211.3 s on c13 (80 %); the resumed builds gave the same placement and routing hashes and WNS as fresh builds.
- **Reports:** 9 % (c10) and 12 % (c13) of a fresh 4-thread build.
- **Not repeated:** the incremental-implementation rows (they need hand edits between runs) and the ILA-from-base rows (the 2026.1
  ILA build uses the IP route of brief 48). Both are withdrawn from published pages.
- **Licence check: changed.** With every licence hidden (empty HOME, no licence variables), Vivado 2026.1 **does not start**:
  `ERROR: Vivado Design Suite cannot be launched because a valid license was not found` (`licence_check.log`). On the earlier release
  it synthesised, placed and routed the three parts from the no-cost edition with no licence file. So 2026.1 needs its (free) Basic
  licence installed, which matters for the cloud image (`cloud/BUILD_AMI.md`).

Steps per build (`stages.txt`), the hash method and the `raw/` layout are as in [results/33_vivado_speed](../33_vivado_speed/README.md);
the rig's `timing_summary.rpt` is cut to its first 160 lines and its `stdout.txt` gzipped, as there.

## Reproduce

The four commands above: Vivado 2026.1, a heavy slot, about two and a half hours for the job list, one build at a time under a 6 GB cap. The wall times depend on the machine and on other load, so a rerun will not match them.

## Known gaps and limits

- One machine, shared with other sessions' jobs that night: wall times carry that load; the 1-thread c10 versus 4-thread c10 comparison shows no measurable gain under it.
- The rig was run at 1 thread only (2 and 4 threads did not fit the 6 GB cap); the earlier figures for them are withdrawn.
- The incremental-implementation and ILA-from-base rows were not repeated and are withdrawn.
- The build-cache saving is against a modelled uncached sequence (one agent run's sequence), not seven uncached runs.
- Licence check: the no-licence case fails to start; the Basic licence must be installed.
