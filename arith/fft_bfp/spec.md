# Block-floating-point FFT (one exponent per frame, W-bit output words, two architectures) (arith/fft_bfp)

Generated by `python -m arith.tools.gen_spec_md` from [spec.yaml](spec.yaml); edit the YAML, not this file. Part of the frequency-domain suite of brief 161 ([../README.md](../README.md)).

The N = 2**LOGN point DFT of frames of complex samples (signed W-bit parts), forward or inverse per frame, with BLOCK FLOATING POINT scaling: the transform runs unscaled (arith/fft with SCALE = 0, W + LOGN bit words inside, so nothing overflows and the only errors are the twiddle and rounding noise of the unscaled core), the frame is held in a two-bank frame store while the largest word is found, and the frame leaves as W-bit words with ONE exponent s for the whole frame: the unscaled transform of the frame is (out_re + j out_im) * 2**s, with s chosen so that the largest part of the frame uses all but one of the W bits. The exponent is a side channel (out_exp, valid with every word of the frame, constant over it); an AXI4-Stream wrapper that carries it on m_axis_tuser is not built (the exponent is a documented side channel). Two architectures behind the same ports: sdf (the streaming radix-2^2 core, one sample per clock) and burst (the memory-based single-butterfly core, full handshakes). This is block floating point at the OUTPUT of a wide unscaled transform, not a per-stage scaling schedule inside the transform: the datapath is W + LOGN bits wide whatever the signal level, which costs memory and DSP width against a per-stage scheme (docs/fft_suite_plan.md section 3).

## Function

- frame: N = 2**LOGN consecutive accepted clocks of in_valid, the first with in_sof; sample n is in_re[n] + j in_im[n], two's complement, W bits each; in_inverse is sampled with in_sof and holds for the frame. The transform and its sign convention are those of arith/fft with SCALE = 0: forward X[k] = sum x[n] exp(-j 2 pi n k / N), inverse exp(+j ...), no 1/N
- output: N words per frame, (out_re, out_im) signed W bits plus out_exp, a signed EW-bit exponent with EW = $clog2(2 * W + LOGN) + 1; out_exp is the same on every word of a frame. The unscaled transform value of output word k is (out_re + j out_im) * 2**out_exp, to within the stated error; a positive exponent means the words were shifted right (information discarded, rounded), zero or negative means no information was discarded (a negative exponent: a small frame was shifted left, exactly, to use the word)
- exponent rule: every part of the core's unscaled output is a (W + LOGN)-bit integer. Let M be the OR over all parts of the frame of x (x >= 0) or -x - 1 (x < 0) and BL its bit length; then s = BL + 1 - W: the smallest shift after which the largest part fits W signed bits. An all-zero frame has BL = 0 and s = 1 - W (the output is zero, the exponent is the extreme negative one); so the exponent ranges from 1 - W to LOGN
- shift: a positive s shifts right, rounding half up when RND = 1 (floor when RND = 0); a negative s shifts left exactly; a positive part that the rounding carries to 2**(W-1) is saturated to 2**(W-1) - 1 (the one place where the output word is wrong by more than half a unit, by one unit)
- ORDER = 0: natural order (X[0], X[1], ... in output position order); ORDER = 1: the core's bit-reversed order (the word at output position p is X[bitrev(p)]). The frame store absorbs the reordering: natural order costs no extra memory here (the store is read at bit-reversed addresses)
- error: the output word error, in units of the unscaled transform, is at most the unscaled core's own error (arith/fft, SCALE = 0: twiddle quantisation and rounding at each stage, measured in arith/fft/verif/results/accuracy.json) plus the final rounding, at most 2**(s-1) (2**s when a part saturates; RND = 0: below 2**s). Relative to the frame's largest part the final rounding is at most 2**-(W-1), about -6.02 * (W - 1) dB; the core's absolute error does not shrink with the signal level, so the SQNR of a QUIET frame is limited by the core, not by the word length: see accuracy.json, which records the error beside the scaled and unscaled modes of arith/fft over N = 8 to 4096
- no overflow is possible: the core is unscaled and wide, the output is normalised from the actual frame. A full-scale corner sample can overflow the core's own (W + LOGN)-bit arithmetic only where it could in arith/fft SCALE = 0 (magnitude at full scale, see arith/fft/spec.md); a magnitude below full scale cannot

Signedness: all data ports are two's complement; out_exp is a signed exponent.

Exactness: bit-exact against the model (arith/fft_bfp/model.py: the unscaled bit-exact core model, the exponent rule above and the normalisation) for every frame within the core's overflow guarantee; against the real DFT the error is bounded as stated in the semantics and measured in verif/results/accuracy.json (worst and rms error and SQNR, beside the scaled and unscaled modes of arith/fft, N = 8 to 4096, input levels from full scale to -60 dB).

Latency: clocks from the clock in which in_sof is asserted to the clock in which out_sof is asserted; throughput is stated per variant.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Constraint | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `LOGN` | min 3 | log2 of the frame length N: 3 to 12 (N = 8 to 4096) |
| `W` | min 4 | input and output part width |
| `TW` | min 4 | twiddle width, 4 to 28 (scale 2**(TW-2)) |
| `RND` | one of [0, 1] | 1: round half up at every shift (stages and the final normalisation); 0: truncate (floor) |
| `ORDER` | one of [0, 1] | 0: natural-order output; 1: bit-reversed output |
| `CMUL` | one of [3, 4] | twiddle multiplier form: 4 real multipliers or 3 (identical results) |
| `TWSTAGES` | one of [1, 2] | twiddle ROM latency |
| `MULSTAGES` | min 0 | register ranks in each complex multiplier, 0 to 3 |

### Intended primitive use

- `sdf`, Artix-7 (xc7a100t): N = 1024, W = TW = 16, CMUL 4, MULSTAGES 3: the twiddle multipliers see operands up to W + LOGN = 26 bits against 16: a 26 x 16 product does not fit one DSP48E1 (25 x 18), so each real multiplier takes 2 DSP blocks (about 4 x 2 x 4 = 32 DSP48E1 for (LOGN - 1) // 2 = 4 complex multipliers; the first stages' narrower words use fewer), delay memory N - 1 words of up to 52 bits (about 2 x the scaled mode), the frame store 2 x 1024 x 52 bits = 104 Kbit (6 RAMB18 + 1 RAMB36 equivalents, about 12 RAMB18), a 43-bit barrel shifter (about 300 LUT); expected Fmax 200 to 300 MHz
  - K26 (xck26): as a7 with DSP48E2 (27 x 18: the 26-bit operand fits, so 4 DSP per complex multiplier again); the frame store in URAM (one 288 Kbit block) or RAMB36; Fmax 350 to 500 MHz expected
- `burst`, Artix-7 (xc7a100t): N = 1024, W = TW = 16: one complex multiplier on up to 26 x 16 bit operands: 2 DSP48E1 per real multiplier, so 8 (CMUL 4); two banks of 512 x 52 bits in the core (about 2 x 26 Kbit) and the frame store 104 Kbit; about 700 to 1100 LUT; expected Fmax 200 to 300 MHz at MULSTAGES 3
  - K26 (xck26): as a7 with DSP48E2 (27 x 18: 4 DSP for the complex multiplier); BRAM or URAM for the store; Fmax 350 to 500 MHz expected

## Interface contract

Written without knowing the design the block will sit in (brief 161, owner decision 24): this is what a user can rely on and must provide.

- Protocol: streaming frames as arith/fft: sdf has no back-pressure (in_valid high for N consecutive clocks per frame, the next frame after no gap or at least N/4 idle clocks, out_ready ignored, in_ready always 1); burst: in_sof only while in_ready is 1, out_ready stalls the output with every output signal held. The exponent out_exp is valid together with out_valid and is the same on all N words of a frame
- Throughput: sdf: one sample per clock continuously, a frame every N clocks (the frame store is two banks, one being written while the other is read). burst: one frame per N + LOGN * (N/2 + MULSTAGES + 3) + N clocks as arith/fft burst; the store adds no time
- Latency: sdf: N - 1 + LOGN + ((LOGN - 1) // 2) * (TWSTAGES + MULSTAGES + 1) + N + 2 (the core's bit-reversed latency, a frame to store, 2 clocks to read) for both orders. burst: N + LOGN * (N/2 + MULSTAGES + 3) + 1 + N + 2
- Reset: synchronous: clears the control state of the core and the store (bank flags, counters); the memories are not cleared. A frame in flight when rst is asserted is lost; no test covers a reset in the middle of a frame
- Clock: one clock, rising edge, no clock-enable
- Stalls: sdf: none (the sink must take every word when presented; in_valid must stay high for N clocks per frame); burst: in_ready low means busy, out_ready low stalls the output and, when both store banks are full, the core

| Variant | Latency (clocks) | Throughput |
|---|---|---|
| `sdf` | `(1<<LOGN)-1+LOGN+((LOGN-1)//2)*(TWSTAGES+MULSTAGES+1)+(1<<LOGN)+2` | 1 sample per clock, a frame every N clocks (gap 0 or at least N/4), continuously |
| `burst` | `(1<<LOGN)+LOGN*((1<<LOGN)//2+MULSTAGES+3)+1+(1<<LOGN)+2` | one frame per N + LOGN * (N/2 + MULSTAGES + 3) + N clocks (load, compute, unload), no overlap |

## Integration

- Port timing: outputs come from registers; the longest paths are the core's complex multiplier (MULSTAGES 3 lets the DSPs absorb their registers), the (W + LOGN + W)-bit barrel shifter and rounding adder before the output register, and the exponent's priority encoder from the stored OR (a register) to the exponent register
- Constraints to carry: one clock, no multicycle paths. The frame store is one simple dual-port memory of 2 x N words of 2 * (W + LOGN) bits (one write port, one read port); the read of a bank never overlaps a write of the same bank (the store is two banks; for sdf the schedule guarantees it as long as the sink takes every word)
- Verification after integration: rerun the packaged harness against your instance ([harness/README.md](harness/README.md)); it needs no other file of this repository.

## What a user could guess wrong

- the exponent is of the whole FRAME, and it is the shift that was applied: value = word * 2**out_exp. A quiet frame has a NEGATIVE exponent (it was shifted left to use the word), a loud frame a positive one. The exponent is not the position of a peak, and it is not an exponent of the INPUT: the input is integers; the exponent belongs to the output relative to the plain unscaled sum
- there is no 1/N anywhere: with the exponent the output equals the unscaled transform (forward and inverse alike), unlike arith/fft SCALE = 1 which divides by N. A consumer that expects the inverse transform of an inverse-transform-of-a-spectrum to give back the original must multiply by 2**out_exp / N
- the words are normalised to the frame, so two frames of different level are NOT in the same scale: compare or accumulate them only after applying (or aligning) their exponents. An all-zero frame has the exponent 1 - W (the extreme), not zero
- the quiet-signal accuracy is limited by the unscaled core's absolute rounding noise, not by the W-bit word: normalising the output cannot recover precision lost inside the transform (a frame at -60 dB of full scale has an SQNR of at least 35.4 dB against at least 85.8 dB for a full-scale one, accuracy.json); a scheme that scales the INPUT up first (a frame buffer in front, not built) or scales per stage would do better and costs more
- the datapath is W + LOGN bits wide whatever the signal: the saving over the unscaled mode is only in the output word, not in the multipliers (the twiddle multipliers see up to W + LOGN bit operands: with W = 16 and N = 4096 that is 28 bits, which does not fit a DSP48E1 25 x 18 multiplier in one block)
- ORDER = 0 natural order costs no reorder buffer here but the output is delayed by one whole frame (the store), so the latency is N + 2 clocks longer than the unscaled arith/fft core's bit-reversed one
- sdf needs the frame to be N consecutive clocks and the gap rule of arith/fft sdf (0 or at least N/4 clocks); out_ready is ignored, a sink that stalls loses words; burst ignores in_sof while in_ready is 0
- saturation: a positive part that the rounding carries to 2**(W-1) is clamped to 2**(W-1) - 1, so one word can be off by one unit; RND = 0 never saturates

## Lean, and what is research

Not attempted. The normalisation is arithmetic on integers (OR of magnitudes, bit length, shift) checked exhaustively against the model at the small points and by the exponent test frames; a proof that the shifted value fits W bits needs the bit-length lemma, which is a candidate for formal/Lean later.
