Specification and evidence

What is specified, and what checks it

The human specifies the requirements and checks that the verification is thorough enough. The agent holds the knowledge to implement and to verify. You can open any of the implementation or the evidence, and you do not have to. Hardware on the board, with attached instruments, is a verification layer of its own: where a requirement depends on something outside the design (an optical link, a network card, a data source) it may be the only way to close it.

Each row is a requirement. Each column is a way of checking it. Every status is computed by code from an evidence file; none is typed. A performance requirement counts as verified only when a measured value lies inside its bound, and the page says whether that was measured on the board, by post-route timing, or in simulation only. A requirement that depends on something outside the design, such as a network card or a second clock, is shown plainly as open until hardware verification has been done. Evidence that is older than the design files it is about is marked stale. Requirements here were drafted from the designs' own documents and none has been agreed by a human yet.
